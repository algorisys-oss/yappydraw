# 0.8.229 — the documentation site returning visitors could not reach (2026-09-03)

## Fixes

- **Every prerendered page served the editor's app shell to returning visitors** (#339).
  `navigateFallback: 'index.html'` answers every navigation from the service worker cache,
  and only `oauth-callback.html` was excluded. So for anyone whose browser had the worker
  installed — everyone except a first-time visitor — `/help/`, `/help/uml/`, `/learn/`,
  `/examples/` and `/founders/` returned the app shell rather than the page.

  On `/help/` and `/examples/` this was invisible: the SPA has client-side routes for both,
  so it recognised the path and rendered its own version. What was lost there is quiet — the
  prerendered HTML, the page title, the canonical and the structured data — so it cost search
  rather than users. `/learn/` and `/founders/` have no such route and showed the canvas.

  All prerendered paths, plus the legal pages, `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt`, are now on the
  navigation denylist and reach the network as they always should have.

- **The vibe-architecting article is unpublished** from `/learn/`. It went live in v0.8.227 as
  a full article — page, sitemap entry, index listing and sidebar link — and is internal
  documentation. The document and its figures stay in the repo.

- **Front matter supports `internal: true`**, filtered once where articles are read so an
  internal document cannot reach the page writer, the sitemap, the index or the sidebar by
  being missed in one of them.

- **`renderHelpDoc` silently discarded unknown front-matter keys.** Its returned `meta` is
  built from an explicit allowlist, so `internal` parsed correctly, never reached the filter,
  and the page published anyway.

## Internal

The service-worker fix was A/B'd against production under identical conditions, with the
worker confirmed to be *controlling* the page rather than merely registered. That distinction
mattered: the first verification reported success on all four paths while no worker was in the
way, and was therefore testing nothing. `verify:deploy` cannot catch this class of bug at all,
since curl never installs a service worker.
